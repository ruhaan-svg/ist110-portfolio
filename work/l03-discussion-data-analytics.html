L03 Data analytics: my muddiest point

Lesson 3, Computing Concepts. Discussion, posted 18 September 2026.

The prompt

Share the most confusing part of descriptive, predictive and prescriptive analytics, then help classmates with theirs.

My post

My muddiest point is the difference between predictive and prescriptive analytics. I understand that descriptive analytics explains what happened and predictive analytics estimates what might happen next. I sometimes get stuck on where predictive ends and prescriptive begins.

For example, if a store predicts that sales will drop next month, that is predictive analytics. If the analysis recommends lowering prices or increasing advertising to respond to the expected drop, that becomes prescriptive analytics. Thinking of it as “What happened?” “What will happen?” and “What should we do?” helped make the differences much clearer.

My replies to classmates

To a classmate who mixed up descriptive and prescriptive analytics

I can see why those two terms are easy to mix up. I think it helps to remember that descriptive focuses on what happened in the past, while prescriptive uses data to recommend what you should do next. The similar names definitely make them more confusing.

To a classmate who explained predictive and prescriptive analytics

I agree with your explanation. Thinking of predictive analytics as showing what might happen and prescriptive analytics as recommending what to do about it helped me understand the difference too. The two work together since the prediction can guide the recommended action.

Reflection

Honestly, early on I treated discussions as a box to tick. My post here was short and my replies were mostly quick agreements. The content was fine, and thinking of it as what happened, what will happen and what should we do actually cleared up predictive versus prescriptive for me.

From Lesson 5 my posts got longer and more structured, partly because I started using AI to help draft them. Looking back, this post is a fair snapshot of where I started.
