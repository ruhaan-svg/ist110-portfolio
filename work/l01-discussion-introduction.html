L01 Introducing myself and Information Systems

Lesson 1, Introduction to Information Science and Technology. Discussion, posted 1 September 2026.

The prompt

Introduce myself, pick the IT discipline that interests me most, research its strengths, limitations and job titles, then reply to classmates.

My post

Hi! My name is Ruhaan and I'm a senior majoring in Finance with a minor in Supply Chain and Information Systems. I spent my first two years at Penn State Berks before transferring to University Park. I'm from Singapore and served in the Navy before starting school. Outside of class, I do photography and go to the gym.

Information Systems is the discipline that interests me most, mainly the MIS side that the reading puts in business schools, where it's more about business and organisational skills than actual computing. I picked it because of something I ran into at my internship this summer in corporate banking. Getting a credit approval through took ages, and the analysis itself was rarely the hard part. The information sat across three different systems that didn't connect, so people spent most of their time chasing it down. Fixing that needs someone who understands the banking side and the systems side, and that's basically what this discipline trains you for.

I also like that it isn't tied to tech companies. Every industry needs it. BLS lists computer systems analysts at a median of $105,850 in 2025 compared to $50,980 for all jobs, and IT is projected to grow faster than average through 2035 (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2026). The downside is you don't go deep on anything technical. For a hard engineering problem, you'd have to bring in someone from CS. You also end up answering for projects you don't really control, since you're coordinating between teams rather than managing one. Job titles I've come across: business systems analyst, IT business analyst, ERP consultant, data analyst, product owner, IT project manager. My strengths are Excel and financial modelling. Right now I'm coding a site in HTML and CSS for the portfolio project, which is new for me. Programming and databases are my weak spots, so hopefully IST 210 helps this semester.

If anyone here has worked in IT already, I'd be curious whether the business-facing analyst role feels safe from automation or like the first thing to go.

Reference

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. (2026, August 27). Computer and information technology occupations. Occupational Outlook Handbook. U.S. Department of Labor. https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/home.htm

My replies to classmates

To a classmate who had built websites with the Divi builder

I'm hand-coding my portfolio in HTML and CSS this semester, so the Divi part stood out. Did you ever hit something it couldn't do, or did it cover everything you needed?

To a classmate planning a career in IT sales

Small thing, but database admin and network admin are on the IT side in the reading rather than IS. For sales you'd probably be looking at sales ops or CRM analyst roles instead. What industry are you trying to sell into?

Reflection

I chose Information Systems because of my internship, where credit approvals stalled while people chased information across three systems that didn't connect. After this semester I still think it is the right fit for me, given my major and what I study, but I would describe it differently now.

It sits between two skill sets: the hard, analytical data skills of IST, and the finance side of turning that data into a conclusion someone can act on. Writing about the discipline's limits was harder than writing about its strengths, because I was criticising the field I want to work in.
