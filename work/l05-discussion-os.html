L05 A love letter to YouTube

Lesson 5, Operating Systems. Discussion, posted 30 September 2026.

The prompt

Write a love letter or a break-up letter to an operating system or app I use a lot, with a short reflection.

My post

Dear YouTube,

Every time I open you "for five minutes", I resurface two hours later knowing how submarines are built and how to fix a bike I don't own. That's love.

You taught me Excel shortcuts before my first model, explained things my lectures couldn't, and kept me company through way too many late nights. You've never judged my watch history. Not once.

You do have your issues though. Two unskippable ads before a 40-second video is a bit much. And asking "Are you still watching?" feels personal when we both know the answer.

Still, I keep coming back. I don't think I'll ever stop.

Yours, one more video at a time,
Ruhaan

Reflection: I picked YouTube because I use it for everything, from learning to procrastinating. Writing this was fun, but also a bit guilt-inducing once I thought about how many hours I've spent on it. It made me realise how much of what I've learned came from free videos rather than a classroom, which says a lot about how big a role tech plays in how we learn now. Honestly, it helped me see that my relationship with YouTube is more useful than I give it credit for, even if it does eat my evenings.

My replies to classmates

To a classmate's love letter to Pinterest

"Imagine things well beyond what my bank account can afford" is painfully accurate. Pinterest really is the app of lovely plans and zero budget.

The beige shoebox line made me laugh too. Every dorm starts out looking like that, so crediting Pinterest for the glow-up feels fair. I also like that you were honest in your reflection about the love being exaggerated. That's probably true for most of us, but it's clear the app has actually shown up for you when it counted.

To a classmate's break-up letter to Instagram

The fact that you picked Instagram because Reels distracted you mid-assignment is honestly the best reflection answer I've read. That's the whole letter proven in one sentence.

I like that you still gave it credit for the laughs and ideas before ending things. It makes the break-up feel real rather than just angry. "We should see other people" had me laughing too.
